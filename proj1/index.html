Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii Photo Collection

One-line summary of what this project does.

Overview

Who Prokudin-Gorskii was, what the glass plate negatives are, and what the program has to do: split the plate into three channels, align them, stack them into RGB.

Approach

Splitting the plate

Equal thirds, top to bottom, in BGR order. Note the plate height isn't always divisible by three.

Scoring a candidate displacement

Exhaustive search over a window of displacements; np.roll to shift. Define L2 and NCC, and say which you use where and why.

Ignoring the borders

Why the film borders wreck the score: they're brighter than the interior in all three channels and differ between them, so they dominate the metric no matter the shift. Scoring on the central portion fixes it. Include the measured before/after.

Coarse-to-fine pyramid

Why exhaustive search fails on the full-size scans: displacements reach ~180 px, so the window would have to be enormous. Recursion: shrink by 2 until the image is small, search wide once, then double the estimate and refine by a couple of pixels at each level on the way back up. Mention blurring before subsampling.

The hard case: Emir of Bukhara

What went wrong: the emir's robe is bright blue, so it is bright in the blue plate and dark in the red one. Correlating raw intensities can't match content whose brightness is inverted — NCC scored −0.23 on his red channel against −0.63 or better on every other plate.

The fix and why it works: green sits between red and blue in the spectrum, so the red and green plates resemble each other far more. Since the green channel has already been moved into blue's frame, anything aligned to it is aligned to blue too.
